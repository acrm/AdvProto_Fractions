# Northgate Setting Canon

## Scope
This document captures the canonical world frame for the Northgate frontier scenario used by Phase Dominion gameplay.
It defines place, era, factions, resources, locations, and the seasonal iteration model in setting terms.

## World and Era
- Northgate is a peripheral city of the Survivor Alliance.
- The Alliance formed after an unnamed magical apocalypse that happened during a period analogous to Earth's Dark Ages.
- The current era is analogous to the Early Renaissance (15th century): relative political calm, growth of trade, craft, cartography, contracts, and proto-scientific institutions.
- Northgate sits on the coast of the Northern Sea, bordered by rocky foothills and mountain approaches.

## Population and Culture
- Northgate was originally founded by ancient dwarves.
- Distant descendants of those dwarves still live in the city as an ethnic minority with strong historical claims and cultural memory.
- Roughly half of the city population is human (multiple cultures).
- The remaining population includes other classic fantasy races and mixed-heritage groups.
- Elves are absent from Northgate by canon, for unresolved lore reasons.

## The Northgate Gate Event
- The Northgate Gates remained sealed for more than one thousand years.
- A few years ago, accidental circumstances caused the seals to break.
- Beyond the Gates lies a newly accessible underground continent:
  - tunnel networks stretching for thousands of miles,
  - vast vault-caverns large enough to host small cities,
  - abandoned dwarven fortress-holds,
  - rich mineral and ore fields,
  - ancient crypts and hidden caches,
  - lairs of cave fauna, monsters, and semi-sapient peoples.

## Strategic Premise
- The core conflict is control over the pace and terms of underworld expansion, not immediate total conquest.
- The main bottlenecks are safe logistics, reliable information, legal legitimacy, and social tolerance for losses.
- Each season represents one expeditionary campaign cycle with political consequences in Northgate.

## Factions (MVP Canon)

### 1) Guild of Grey Lanterns (Player)
- Public identity: contracted guides, intelligence brokers, and operational mediators.
- Long-term ambition: become indispensable for access and coordination beyond the Gates.
- Strengths: agility, adaptable routing, intelligence handling.
- Weaknesses: fragile base resources, reputation dependence.

### 2) Northgate Dwarven Assembly
- Public identity: custodians of founder rights, records, and engineering traditions.
- Long-term ambition: recover ancestral holds and formalize legal precedence underground.
- Strengths: heritage maps, hold-culture discipline, reconstruction capability.
- Weaknesses: internal conservatism, political friction with newer powers.

### 3) Harbor Patricians League
- Public identity: merchant houses, insurers, and port syndics.
- Long-term ambition: monopoly over charters, credit, and expedition supply law.
- Strengths: finance, procurement, contract leverage.
- Weaknesses: exposed to scandals, vulnerable to route disruption.

### 4) Concord of Free Companies
- Public identity: mercenary captains and convoy security contractors.
- Long-term ambition: make armed escort and force projection a mandatory paid standard.
- Strengths: tactical force, fast enforcement, disciplined detachments.
- Weaknesses: costly operations, legitimacy risk if violence escalates.

### 5) Lantern Church and Scriptoria
- Public identity: clerical archives, scribal scholarship, relic interpretation.
- Long-term ambition: control what knowledge is validated, archived, and publicly sanctioned.
- Strengths: narrative legitimacy, scholarly infrastructure, ritual authority.
- Weaknesses: slow adaptation, exposure to proof-of-fraud events.

### 6) Tidebound Syndicate
- Public identity: denied by all, present in every dock district.
- Long-term ambition: make official institutions dependent on black-market channels.
- Strengths: smuggling routes, covert access, deniable assets.
- Weaknesses: high exposure volatility, unstable loyalty chains.

### 7) Deepkind Envoys
- Public identity: emissaries of multiple semi-sapient underworld peoples.
- Long-term ambition: shape surface expansion toward favorable zones and protect taboo regions.
- Strengths: local terrain knowledge, adaptive diplomacy, environmental asymmetry.
- Weaknesses: fragmented coordination, divergent clan interests.

## Seasonal Resource Set
Each faction begins every season with a concrete stock profile.

### Operational resources
- Provisions: food, medicine, expedition consumables.
- Tools and Powder: engineering kits, supports, blasting resources.
- Coin and Credit: liquid funds and debt capacity.
- Claims and Charters: legal rights to routes, shafts, and outposts.

### Informational and strategic resources
- Intel: maps, reports, rumors, interception products.
- Relics: high-impact artifacts and pre-collapse mechanisms.

### Socio-political constraints
- Public Order: social tolerance, panic pressure, civic trust.
- Exposure: accumulated reputational and political vulnerability.

## Key Locations
- Northgate Harbor: maritime logistics, insurance, tariffs, hiring markets.
- Stone Crown District: old dwarven quarter, guild halls, archive houses.
- The Sealed Stair: mountain ascent corridor to the Gate bastion.
- Gate Bastion: militarized checkpoint and expedition staging ring.
- The Thousand Miles: primary deep transit artery.
- Vault-Cities: giant cavern systems suitable for settlement nodes.
- Fallen Holds: abandoned dwarven fortress complexes.
- Veinfields: major mineral and ore clusters.
- Crypt Belts: high-risk relic and burial zones.
- Beast Warrens: concentrated predator and monster habitats.
- Deepkind Marches: semi-sapient frontier polities and exchange points.
- Saltwind Cliffs: rocky coastal infrastructure belt outside central districts.

## Seasonal Iteration Model (Setting-Locked)
- One season contains exactly 5 iterations.
- At season start, each faction sets one full seasonal movement vector toward its long-term objective.
- Each iteration executes exactly 20% of that original vector.
- After each iteration, the world emits events and news reports visible to other factions.
- In the baseline model, non-player factions do not replan mid-season.
- Only the player can adjust the remaining vector between iterations.
- Completed vector portion is locked and cannot be rewritten.

Remaining-vector authority for player:
- Iteration 2 controls the remaining 0.8 of original vector.
- Iteration 3 controls the remaining 0.6.
- Iteration 4 controls the remaining 0.4.
- Iteration 5 controls the remaining 0.2.

## Information Output Tone
Iteration output should read as diegetic Northgate signals, not abstract system text:
- dock bulletins,
- patrol dispatch notes,
- market rumors,
- clerical notices,
- underworld contact reports.

## Tone and Scope Constraints
- Keep conflict primarily logistical, political, and intelligence-driven.
- Avoid defaulting to high-fantasy battlefield spectacle as the main loop.
- Treat magic as rare, costly, and politically sensitive.
- Keep the unresolved absence of elves as a long-form lore hook.

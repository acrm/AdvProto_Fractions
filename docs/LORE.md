# Lore

## Premise
Nor's Gate is the only city standing over the single known open descent into a continent-sized under-country. What lies below is not a dungeon in the adventuring-room sense, but an entire buried frontier: minefields of saltglass, sealed archives, shrines sunk into black water, caravan roads carved into cavern walls, and old empires collapsed into monster-haunted infrastructure.

The surface powers call it opportunity. The people who live near the gate call it pressure. Every expedition that returns with relics, survey maps, miracle-water, or military leverage makes Nor's Gate richer, harsher, and harder to govern.

## Tone
- D&D-like fantasy filtered through frontier colonial tension.
- Wild-west law, company charters, hard bargains, dirty sanctity, and armed logistics.
- Atmospheric but concise storytelling: short reports, rumors, briefings, and aftermaths rather than long exposition.

## Core Conflict
Five major powers compete to shape the under-country in their own image:
- political authority wants taxable order,
- religious authority wants sacred legitimacy,
- economic authority wants extractive control,
- scientific authority wants monopolies over knowledge,
- military authority wants security and coercive necessity.

Between them survives the Grey Lantern Guild, a small free company of adventurers, fixers, and contract crews trying to seize a permanent place in the frontier without being absorbed, ruined, or sanctified by the larger powers.

## Why Nor's Gate Matters
- It is the only open gate.
- Every major supply line converges there.
- Every permit, sermon, debt, escort contract, and excavation charter passes through it.
- Whoever dominates Nor's Gate can shape the terms of descent for everyone else.

## Player Fantasy
The player is not a sovereign empire. The player is the Grey Lantern Guild: small, deniable, mobile, and opportunistic. The guild survives by taking contracts, breaking contracts, triangulating between factions, and turning local advantage into lasting leverage.
